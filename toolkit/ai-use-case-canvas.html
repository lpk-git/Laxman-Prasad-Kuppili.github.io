TK-07 Canvas AI

AI Use-Case Canvas

Provide a structured way to evaluate an AI idea before deciding whether AI should actually be used.

1. WHAT IS THIS?

The AI Use-Case Canvas is a one-page framework designed to critically evaluate potential AI applications. It helps teams move past the "AI hype" to concretely define what the AI will do, how it will be validated, and where human judgement must remain in the loop.

2. THE CANVAS

1. BUSINESS PROBLEM

  • What problem are we solving?

2. USERS

  • Who benefits from the AI capability?

3. WHY AI?

  • Why is AI appropriate?
  • Could conventional automation solve the problem more reliably?

4. INPUT / CONTEXT

  • What information does AI require?

5. AI CAPABILITY

  • What should AI do? (e.g. summarise, classify, extract, generate, analyse, identify patterns, answer questions)

6. EXPECTED OUTPUT

  • What should AI produce?

7. HUMAN IN THE LOOP

  • Who validates the output?
  • Where is human judgement required?

8. ACTION

  • What happens after AI produces the result?

9. GUARDRAILS

  • What must AI not do?

10. DATA & PRIVACY

  • What information is being provided to the AI?
  • Is sensitive information involved?

11. VALIDATION

  • How will output quality be checked?

12. FAILURE MODES

  • What could AI get wrong?
  • What happens when confidence is insufficient?

13. SUCCESS MEASURES

  • How will we determine whether AI adds meaningful value?

14. AI vs AUTOMATION DECISION GUIDE

If the task is:

predictable + rule-based + deterministic

→ Consider conventional automation.

If the task involves:

language + interpretation + summarisation + unstructured information

→ AI may add value.

If both are needed:

A hybrid approach is highly effective.

→ Automation → AI → Human Review → Action

3. EXAMPLE: AI-Assisted Sprint Insight Generation

Problem & Users

Problem: Scrum Masters spend hours manually correlating Jira data to find bottlenecks.
Users: Delivery Teams & Scrum Masters.

Why AI? & Input

Why AI: Hard to code rigid rules for nuanced team behaviors; requires text interpretation.
Input: Raw Jira sprint data, ticket comments, transitions.

AI Capability & Output

Capability: Identify patterns and summarise blockers.
Output: A concise bulleted list of potential risks and themes.

Human In The Loop & Action

HITL: The Scrum Master reviews observations. AI does not make delivery decisions or evaluate people.
Action: Discuss verified themes in the retrospective.

Guardrails, Privacy & Failure Modes

Guardrails: AI must not recommend firing or punitive actions.
Privacy: Redact personal names/emails before sending to the LLM.
Failure Modes: Hallucinating blockers that do not exist. Over-reliance on AI by the Scrum Master.
