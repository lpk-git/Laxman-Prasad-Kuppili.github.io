TK-03 Reference Delivery

Flow Metrics Cheat Sheet

Provide a concise practical reference for understanding delivery flow and measuring predictability.

1. CORE FLOW METRICS

Metric Definition Question Answered
1. CYCLE TIME Time from work starting to work finishing. "How long does work typically take once started?"
2. LEAD TIME Time from request/commitment to delivery, depending on the measurement boundary being used. "How long does a customer/request wait for delivery?"
3. THROUGHPUT Number of work items completed during a given period. "How much work is actually finishing?"
4. WORK IN PROGRESS (WIP) Work started but not yet completed. "How much work are we carrying simultaneously?"
5. WORK ITEM AGE How long an unfinished work item has already been in progress. "Which current items may require attention?"

2. ADVANCED FLOW CONCEPTS

6. FLOW EFFICIENCY

Active working time compared with total elapsed flow time. Measuring flow efficiency often reveals that work spends more time waiting than being actively worked on. Identifying and reducing waiting time often exposes significant opportunities for improvement.

7. CUMULATIVE FLOW DIAGRAM (CFD)

Shows how work accumulates across workflow states over time. A CFD can visually help identify growing queues, bottlenecks, and expanding WIP before they severely impact delivery speed.

3. READ METRICS TOGETHER

Flow metrics are connected. Changing one aspect of the system reliably influences others. For example, high WIP cascades into lower predictability:

High WIP
↓
Longer queues
↓
Increasing work-item age
↓
Longer cycle time
↓
Lower predictability

4. AVOID MISUSE

  • Do not use individual-level flow metrics to rank people.
  • Do not optimise one metric in isolation.
  • Understand workflow boundaries (where does the clock start and stop?).
  • Compare trends and context rather than blindly targeting arbitrary numbers.
